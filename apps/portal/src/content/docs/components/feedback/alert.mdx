## Anatomy

All parts of the `Alert` can be imported and composed as required:

```typescript jsx
<Alert.Root>
  <Alert.Title />
  <Alert.Description />
  <Alert.Link />
</Alert.Root>
```

- **Root** — the container. Its `theme` (`info` default, `success`, `warning`, `danger`) sets the color and the leading status icon automatically. Add `dismissible` for a close button, or `expandable` to collapse long content behind a "Show more" toggle.
- **Title** _(optional)_ — a short heading for the alert.
- **Description** — the alert's message; the main body text.
- **Link** _(optional)_ — a single action link, rendered with a trailing icon, for a follow-up like "Learn more".

The status icon is chosen from the theme (info, success, warning, danger) — you don't pass it yourself.

## Best practices

**Do**

- Use `Alert` for a message tied to the surrounding content or a recent action — inline, in place, and staying until it's resolved or dismissed.
- Match the `theme` to the message: `danger` for errors and blocked actions, `warning` for risks, `success` for confirmations, `info` for neutral context.
- Lead with the outcome and, when there's a fix, point to it — a clear `Alert.Description` and, if useful, one `Alert.Link` to the next step.
- Use `expandable` for long messages (like policy text or stack traces) so the alert doesn't dominate the layout, and `dismissible` for advisories people can safely clear.

**Don't**

- Don't use `Alert` for transient, auto-dismissing feedback — use a toast — or for a feed of past notifications, where [Alert Item](/components/data-display/alert-item/) fits better.
- Don't stack several alerts competing for attention; consolidate or prioritize the most important one.
- Don't rely on the theme color alone to carry meaning — the text should state the situation clearly.
- Don't make an error alert `dismissible` if the underlying problem still blocks the user.

## Content

- Write the `Description` in plain language: what happened, why it matters, and what to do next.
- Keep the `Title` short and specific when you use one; it's optional and often the description alone is enough.
- Use sentence case throughout, and give any `Alert.Link` a verb-led label ("Learn more", "View repository").
- For long technical detail (URLs, tokens, policy text), rely on `expandable` rather than trimming information people may need to copy.

## Accessibility

`Alert.Root` renders with `role="alert"`, so assistive tech announces it as soon as it appears — this makes it well suited to conveying important, time-sensitive messages.

### Keyboard

- The alert body itself isn't focusable; its interactive parts are.
- The `Alert.Link`, the `dismissible` close button, and the `expandable` "Show more" toggle are each native controls in the tab order, activated with **Enter** (and **Space** for the buttons).

### Labeling

- The close button carries a translated `aria-label` ("Close alert") and the expandable toggle uses `aria-expanded` to expose its open/closed state.
- The content sits in a labeled region (`role="region"`, "Alert content"), and the leading status icon is decorative — meaning comes from the text, so state the situation in the `Description`.

### Focus & states

- The link, close, and expand controls each show the standard focus ring (`:focus-visible`) via the `--cn-focus` token.
- Dismissing an alert removes it from the DOM entirely; expanding/collapsing toggles a fade overlay over the clipped content.

### Known limitations

- **`role="alert"` announces on mount, not on update.** An alert rendered on page load may be announced immediately or missed, depending on timing; for a message that must be heard, render the alert in response to the action rather than having it already present.
- **Focus isn't moved to the alert.** When an alert appears after an action, focus stays where it was; if the alert blocks progress, move focus to it (or its first control) yourself.
- **Dismissing doesn't manage focus.** After the close button removes the alert, focus is not returned to a sensible neighbor, so keyboard users can lose their place.
