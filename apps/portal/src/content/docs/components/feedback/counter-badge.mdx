The `CounterBadge` component is used to highlight and display numerical values or simple count indicators. It is designed to be non-interactive (read-only) and supports different color themes.

## Anatomy

`CounterBadge` is a single, self-contained element — there are no parts to compose:

```typescript jsx
<CounterBadge theme="info">42</CounterBadge>
```

- It renders one `<div>` styled by the `variant` (outline or secondary) and `theme` (default, info, success, danger) you pass, and shows its `children` as-is — a number, a short string like `99+`, or a signed value like `-123`.
- It's read-only by design: the props that would make it interactive or reassign its semantics — `role`, `onClick`, `tabIndex`, `aria-readonly`, `color` — are removed from its type, so it stays a plain, non-interactive indicator.
- Any remaining `HTMLAttributes` (including `className` and `aria-*`) pass straight through to that div.

## Best practices

{/* TODO: editorial — please review against product guidance. */}

**Do**

- Use a counter badge to surface a count or short value next to the thing it describes — a tab, a list heading, a menu item.
- Keep the content to a compact number or short token (`8`, `+99`, `99+`), and cap large numbers (e.g. `99+`) so the badge stays small.
- Choose the `theme` for meaning: `default`/`info` for a neutral count, `success` for a positive change, `danger` for a problem count.

**Don't**

- Don't put a counter badge on something people are meant to click _through the badge_ — it's non-interactive; make the surrounding element the control.
- Don't use it for long text or full labels; reach for [Tag](/components/feedback/tag/) or [Status Badge](/components/feedback/status-badge/) when you need words.
- Don't rely on theme color alone to carry meaning — see the accessibility notes.

## Content

{/* TODO: editorial — please review against product guidance. */}

- Keep it to a number or a short count token; avoid words and punctuation beyond what a count needs (`+`, `-`, `%`).
- Pick a consistent overflow convention (e.g. `99+`) and use it everywhere counts can get large.

## Accessibility

`CounterBadge` is a visual, non-interactive indicator: it renders its content as plain text inside a `<div>` with no semantic role of its own.

### Labeling

- The badge's content is read inline as text, but nothing ties it to what it counts — a bare `3` gives no context on its own. Make sure the surrounding label supplies the meaning ("Errors 3"), or add an `aria-label` on the badge (or its container) that spells it out.

### Focus & states

- The badge isn't focusable and has no interactive states — it never enters the tab order. When the count belongs to an interactive element, put the behavior and focus on that element, not the badge.

### Known limitations

- **A changing count isn't announced.** The badge is a plain `<div>`, not a live region (`role="status"`/`aria-live`), so screen-reader users aren't notified when the number updates. If announcing the change matters, wrap it in your own live region.
- **Theme color isn't meaning.** `success` and `danger` convey improvement/concern only through color. Keep the value itself explicit (a sign like `+`/`-`, or an adjacent label) so the meaning survives without color perception.
