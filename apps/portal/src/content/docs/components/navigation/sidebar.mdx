The `Sidebar` component is a responsive, collapsible navigation surface for an application. It expands and collapses on desktop, slides in as an off-canvas panel on mobile, and supports nested submenus, badges, tooltips, per-item actions, and a footer profile menu.

It's a compound component with parts like `Sidebar.Provider`, `Sidebar.Root`, `Sidebar.Trigger`, `Sidebar.Content`, `Sidebar.Header`, `Sidebar.Footer`, `Sidebar.Group`, `Sidebar.Item`, `Sidebar.MenuSubItem`, `Sidebar.Popover`, `Sidebar.Rail`, `Sidebar.Inset`, and more.

## Anatomy

The `Sidebar` is composed of nested parts that together form a complete navigation surface. `Sidebar.Provider` holds the open/collapsed state and must wrap everything.

```jsx
<Sidebar.Provider>
  <Sidebar.Root>
    <Sidebar.Header />
    <Sidebar.Content>
      <Sidebar.Group>
        <Sidebar.Item>
          <Sidebar.MenuSubItem />
        </Sidebar.Item>
      </Sidebar.Group>
      <Sidebar.Separator />
    </Sidebar.Content>
    <Sidebar.Footer />
    <Sidebar.Rail />
  </Sidebar.Root>
  <Sidebar.Trigger />
  <Sidebar.Inset />
</Sidebar.Provider>
```

- **`Sidebar.Provider`** — owns the expanded/collapsed state (via `open` / `onOpenChange`, or uncontrolled with `defaultOpen`) and shares it through context. State is also persisted to a cookie so it survives reloads.
- **`Sidebar.Root`** — the container that renders the sidebar on desktop and switches to a slide-in `Sheet` on mobile. `side` places it on the left or right.
- **`Sidebar.Content`** — the scrollable region for groups and items (`role="menu"`); **`Sidebar.Header`** / **`Sidebar.Footer`** frame it.
- **`Sidebar.Group`** — a labeled cluster of items (`role="group"`); **`Sidebar.Item`** is the individual entry, with optional icon/logo/avatar, badge, actions, submenu, or dropdown.
- **`Sidebar.MenuSubItem`** — a nested link revealed when an item with children expands; **`Sidebar.Popover`** shows nested items in a flyout instead.
- **`Sidebar.Trigger`** / **`Sidebar.Rail`** / **`Sidebar.ToggleMenuButton`** — three ways to toggle collapse; **`Sidebar.Inset`** is the main content area that sits beside the sidebar.

## Best practices

{/* TODO: editorial — please review against product guidance. */}

**Do**

- Group related destinations with `Sidebar.Group`, and label groups once you have more than one so the structure is scannable.
- Give every `Sidebar.Item` a clear `title` — it's the label shown when expanded and the tooltip shown when collapsed.
- Provide a way to toggle the sidebar (`Sidebar.Trigger`, `Sidebar.Rail`, or `Sidebar.ToggleMenuButton`) so people can reclaim space.
- Use `Sidebar.MenuSkeleton` while a group's items are loading, matching the number of rows you expect.

**Don't**

- Don't overload a single item — `badge`, `actionMenuItems`, `dropdownMenuContent`, `withRightIndicator`, and a submenu (`children`) are mutually exclusive, so pick the one that fits.
- Don't nest more than one level of submenu; use `Sidebar.Popover` when a flyout reads better than an inline expand.
- Don't rely on the collapsed icon alone for unfamiliar destinations — keep titles meaningful so the tooltips are useful.

## Accessibility

### Keyboard

- Items, links, and the toggle controls are standard focusable controls, reachable and activatable from the keyboard. Submenu triggers respond to **Enter** / **Space**.
- Note: a `⌘B` / `Ctrl+B` shortcut is **not** currently wired up — toggle via `Sidebar.Trigger`, `Sidebar.Rail`, or `Sidebar.ToggleMenuButton`.

### Labeling

- `Sidebar.Content` exposes `role="menu"`, `Sidebar.Group` exposes `role="group"`, and items render as `role="menuitem"`, so the structure is conveyed to assistive tech.
- When the sidebar is collapsed, each item shows a tooltip with its `title`. `Sidebar.Trigger` and `Sidebar.Rail` carry their own accessible labels.

### Focus & states

- Disabled items render with `aria-disabled` and aren't interactive. A closed submenu is hidden from assistive tech (`aria-hidden`) until it expands.
