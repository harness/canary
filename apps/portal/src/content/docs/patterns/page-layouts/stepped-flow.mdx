A full page that walks someone through a task one step at a time. A step rail on the left shows progress, the current step fills the content area, and a footer holds Cancel, Back and Continue. In product code this is `StepperWizard`, with a `Stepper` for the step rail beside the step content and a `Button` footer.

## Example

The step rail is a `Stepper`, with the current step filled. The first step has no Back button, so Cancel sits on the left of the footer and Continue on the right.

## Anatomy

1. **Step rail.** A vertical `Stepper` listing the steps in order. Done steps show a check, the current step is filled, and later steps are outlined.
2. **Step header.** The step title and a one-line description of what to do.
3. **Step content.** The fields for the step, kept to a readable width.
4. **Footer.** Cancel on the left. Back and the primary button on the right, separated from the content by a divider.

## Guidelines

- Use a stepped flow for setup and creation tasks with several form-heavy steps. For one or two short steps, use a single page or a drawer.
- Keep to 3 to 6 steps. Name each step with a short noun, such as Details or Review.
- Always end with a Review step that summarises the choices before anything is created.
- Show Back on every step except the first. Do not show it on the first step.
- Label the primary button Continue on every step but the last. On the last step name the outcome, such as Create service.
- Keep Cancel on the left of the footer on every step so exiting is always possible.
- Validate a step before moving on, and show errors beside the fields. Show a loading state on the primary button while it advances.
- Let people go back without losing what they entered.
- Fill the page height. The step content scrolls inside itself while the rail and footer stay in place.
